Adult Partner and Peer Influence

What's Missing

Research on how romantic partners and adult peer networks influence fan origination or intensification. Childhood socialisation literature covers parental and sibling influence thoroughly, but the equivalent dynamics in adult relationships — a partner introducing someone to their team, friend groups pulling someone into fandom, workplace culture effects — lack systematic study.

Why It Matters

For adult fan origination (a documented gap), partner and peer influence may be a primary mechanism. Understanding this pathway matters for sports properties targeting couples, social groups, and workplace audiences. It also matters for understanding gendered patterns of fan development, given historical gender imbalances in core fandom.

Partial Coverage

sources/crawford-2003-career-supporter — The Manchester Storm case study documents that adults enter fandom through:

  • Work colleagues: Social contexts where fandom provides currency
  • Romantic partners: Accompanying fan partner to games
  • Entertainment seeking: Friend groups seeking shared leisure activity

Crawford identifies these as entry routes but does not examine the mechanism by which social network influence produces conversion. How does a partner's fandom become shared fandom? When does workplace exposure become personal identification?

sources/delia-katz-2019-becoming-fans-abroad — Documents peer influence (6 of 22 participants) as an adolescent socialisation agent, primarily via participation in soccer (playing with friends). This is peer influence but at adolescent life stage, not adult.

The study also shows that prior general sport fandom (being fans of other sports before travel) may predispose individuals to acquire new specific fandom. This suggests existing sport-fan identity may be a facilitating condition for partner/peer influence.

What remains unaddressed:

  • The mechanism by which romantic partner fandom becomes shared fandom
  • Whether partner-introduced fans achieve equivalent identification to self-originated fans
  • Gender dynamics in partner influence (who introduces whom? how does this differ by sport?)
  • Workplace fan culture effects on individual origination
  • Friend group dynamics in adult fan acquisition (beyond adolescence)

Suggested Reading Directions

  • Relationship research on shared leisure and interest convergence
  • Brand community literature on member-get-member dynamics
  • Gender and sports fandom research
  • Research on women's pathways into male-dominated fan communities
  • Social network effects in consumer behaviour
  • Couple identity and shared consumption research
