BIRGing and CORFing

Definition

BIRGing (Basking In Reflected Glory): The tendency to associate oneself with successful others (teams, groups, individuals) to enhance self-esteem. Manifests in use of "we" language after wins ("we won"), wearing team apparel after victories, publicly associating with winning teams.

CORFing (Cutting Off Reflected Failure): The tendency to distance oneself from unsuccessful others to protect self-esteem. Manifests in use of "they" language after losses ("they lost"), reduced display of team affiliation after defeats, psychological distancing from losing teams.

Both concepts originated in social psychology research by Cialdini et al. (1976).

Theoretical Basis

Rooted in self-esteem maintenance and impression management. People are motivated to maintain positive self-regard; associating with winners enhances self-image while association with losers threatens it. BIRGing and CORFing are strategies for managing the self-esteem implications of group outcomes.

Connects to Social Identity Theory: if the team is part of one's social identity, team outcomes affect self-esteem directly.

How It Works

  • After team victories, fans are more likely to wear team apparel, use "we" language, and publicly claim fandom
  • After defeats, weakly identified fans distance themselves; highly identified fans cannot easily CORF because the team is too central to their identity
  • BIRGing/CORFing asymmetry: highly identified fans BIRG strongly but cannot CORF; lowly identified fans can do both
  • The inability to CORF is what makes high identification psychologically costly — you cannot escape the pain of losses

Key Sources

  • Cialdini et al. (1976) — original BIRGing research with college football fans
  • sources/wann-1993-ssis — demonstrates highly identified fans show ego-protective attributions; inability to CORF
  • sources/hunt-bristol-bashaw-1999-fan-typology — uses BIRGing specifically to explain "temporary fans" who engage during success then disengage
  • Snyder, Lassegard, & Ford (1986) — CORFing research

Contested Aspects

  • Whether BIRGing/CORFing are conscious impression management strategies or automatic self-esteem regulation
  • Whether these patterns are universal or culturally variable
  • Relationship to identification: is inability to CORF a consequence of high identification, or part of its definition?

Relationship to Adjacent Concepts

  • concepts/team-identification — BIRGing/CORFing are downstream consequences of identification level
  • Self-serving attributions — related ego-protective mechanism
  • Impression management — broader framework within which BIRGing/CORFing sit

Gaps

  • Little research on deliberate BIRGing as fan acquisition strategy (can you get people to BIRG their way into fandom?)
  • Whether BIRGing with successful teams can be a pathway to genuine identification
  • Cross-cultural validity of the phenomena

Applied Note

BIRGing suggests that team success creates opportunities for fan acquisition — people want to associate with winners. But BIRGing alone does not create the deep identification that prevents CORFing. Properties that attract "bandwagon" fans via success may find those fans CORF away when performance declines. The strategic question is whether initial BIRGing can be converted into genuine identification before the losses come.
