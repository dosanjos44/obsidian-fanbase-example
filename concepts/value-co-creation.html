Value Co-Creation

Definition

Value co-creation is the process by which value is produced through the active participation of consumers in the consumption experience, rather than embedded in products by producers and delivered to passive recipients. In sport fandom, co-creation describes how fans and brands develop meaning together through interaction, user-generated content, and community participation — particularly enabled by social media platforms.

The concept is central to Service-Dominant Logic (Vargo & Lusch 2004) and provides the mechanism through which relationship marketing operates.

Theoretical Basis

Goods-Dominant view: Value is created by the producer, embedded in the product, and transferred to the consumer via transaction. The consumer receives value; the producer creates it.

Service-Dominant view: Value is not embedded in products. Organisations offer "value propositions" which consumers then "evaluate in their own terms" (Ballantyne & Varey 2008). Value emerges through the consumption process — it is co-created by the interaction between provider and consumer.

In Vargo & Lusch's formulation: "The customer is always a co-creator of value" (2004). This is not a contingent claim about some customers; it is a foundational premise about how exchange works.

Applied to social media: Social media platforms operationalise co-creation by enabling:

  • User-generated content (fans produce, not just consume)
  • Many-to-many interaction (fans engage with each other, not just with the brand)
  • Community formation (value emerges from collective meaning-making)
  • Ongoing exchange (relationships, not transactions)

Co-Creation in Sport Fan Contexts

Filo et al. (2015) identified co-creation as the distinguishing feature of social media in sport:

  1. Fan-produced content: Match commentary, reaction videos, memes, fan art, supporter group content — value created by fans that the organisation could not produce alone

  2. Community interaction: Fan-to-fan discussion, debate, inside jokes, shared narratives — meaning that emerges from collective participation

  3. Participation in campaigns: User-generated content campaigns, hashtag challenges, fan voting, content curation — designed invitations for co-creation

  4. Behind-the-scenes access: When athletes and teams share authentic, personal content and fans respond/engage, the meaning of "access" is co-created through interaction, not delivered as content

The Engagement-Co-Creation Connection

Brodie et al. (2011, 2013) position psychological engagement as the mechanism driving co-creation: "the engine room driving the formation of meaningful relationships between consumers and brands, communities of consumers, and brands and stakeholders" (cited in Filo et al. 2015, p. 3).

Engagement → Co-creation → Relationship → Value

Yoshida et al. (2014) define engagement as multidimensional — cognitive, affective, and behavioural. Social media enables all three dimensions: thinking about content (cognitive), feeling connection through content (affective), and participating through likes/comments/shares/creation (behavioural).

Contested Aspects

  • Not all interaction is co-creation: Passive scrolling, even liking or brief viewing, may not constitute the active meaning-making that co-creation implies. The concept can be stretched to cover any engagement, diluting its analytical power.

  • Power asymmetry: "Co-creation" implies shared agency, but platforms and brands control the architecture (algorithms, features, policies). Fans create within constraints set by others. The "co" may overstate equality.

  • Quality variation: Not all fan-generated content or participation creates value for the organisation or community. Some user-generated content is low-quality, off-brand, or harmful. Co-creation is not inherently positive.

  • Measurement difficulty: If value is subjectively determined by participants, how do organisations know whether co-creation is succeeding? Metrics remain contested.

  • What kind of value?: Co-creation may produce commercial value (engagement metrics, content assets), social value (community belonging), or psychological value (identity, meaning). These are not equivalent, and the literature often conflates them.

Relationship to Adjacent Concepts

  • concepts/service-dominant-logic — co-creation is the central mechanism within S-D logic; the process through which value is produced

  • concepts/psychological-continuum-model — co-creation may be the mechanism that facilitates movement from attraction to attachment. If the attraction→attachment transition requires personal meaning and identity-linking, co-creative participation may provide this — fans who create are invested in ways passive consumers are not

  • concepts/fan-socialisation — co-creation through social media may function as a modern socialising agent, providing the "active immersion" that Delia & Katz (2019) identified as necessary for adult conversion. Creating content and participating in community is socialisation, not just consumption

  • concepts/fandom-de-escalation — the breakdown of co-creation (feeling unheard, excluded, ignored) may contribute to iterative decline. Martin & Goldman (2015) found that unanswered complaints accelerated detachment

  • concepts/optimal-distinctiveness — Delia & Katz showed that adult conversion requires satisfying both belonging and uniqueness. Co-creation may provide both: participation creates belonging; distinctive community expression provides uniqueness

Gaps

  • Empirical test in fan development context: Does co-creative engagement actually produce attachment or allegiance? The theoretical claim is plausible but untested in sport fandom research.

  • Co-creation and fan types: Does co-creation produce different types of fans? A fan who co-creates with a commercial brand campaign may develop consumer-type connection; a fan who co-creates within a supporter community may develop traditional-type connection. The relationship is untheorised.

  • Platform effects: Algorithms increasingly mediate what content users see and what interactions occur. Does algorithmic curation undermine or enable co-creation? S-D logic and co-creation theory predate the algorithmic feed era.

  • Negative co-creation: What happens when fans co-create content or meaning that conflicts with organisational interests (criticism, parody, opposition campaigns)? Co-creation is not inherently aligned with brand goals.

  • Cross-cultural variation: Consumer participation norms and meanings of "co-creation" may differ across cultures. The framework emerges from Western marketing scholarship.

Applied Note

Co-creation provides the conceptual bridge between social media engagement metrics and fan development outcomes. The question is not "did fans see the content?" but "did fans participate in creating meaning?"

Designing for Co-Creation

Broadcast Approach Co-Creative Approach
Post highlight video Ask fans to submit their angles/reactions
Announce news Invite fans to speculate/discuss before announcement
Share match stats Ask fans to interpret, debate, contribute context
Deliver brand messaging Amplify fan-created content that expresses brand values
Curate "official" narrative Enable community to develop shared narratives

The Fan Development Connection

The reason co-creation matters for fan development is not commercial (though it produces content and engagement). The reason is psychological: creating meaning together produces attachment that passive consumption does not.

The PCM's attraction→attachment transition requires personal meaning and identity-linking. A fan who watches content remains a consumer. A fan who contributes to community discussion, creates content, participates in collective expression — that fan is investing identity in the relationship.

This aligns with Delia & Katz (2019)'s finding that adult conversion requires "active immersion" — not passive exposure. Social media co-creation may provide domestic-context active immersion that travel provides in international contexts.

The Measurement Shift

Moving from vanity metrics to co-creation metrics:

Vanity Metric Co-Creation Metric
Followers Community participants
Impressions Content creators
Likes Discussion contributors
Reach Meaning generated
Shares Narratives adopted

This shift is harder to operationalise but more aligned with what actually produces fan attachment. A brand with high reach but no participation is broadcasting. A brand with lower reach but active co-creation is building relationships.

The Structural Warning

Co-creation is a mechanism, not a guarantee. The question of what kind of fan is being developed through co-creation remains. A fan co-creating with a merchandise campaign develops consumer-type attachment. A fan co-creating within a community-rooted supporter group develops traditional-type attachment. The platform enables both; the content and context determine the outcome.

Organisations pursuing co-creation must ask: co-creation of what? Around what values? Within what kind of community? S-D logic does not answer these questions — it only explains why participation produces value. The character of that value depends on how co-creation is designed and facilitated.
