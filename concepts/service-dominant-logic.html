Service-Dominant Logic (S-D Logic)

Definition

Service-Dominant Logic (S-D logic) is a marketing paradigm that challenges the traditional "goods-dominant" view of exchange. Proposed by Vargo & Lusch (2004), S-D logic argues that value is not embedded in products or delivered by producers; rather, value is co-created through the process of consumption and interaction between providers and consumers. In sport management, S-D logic provides the theoretical frame for understanding how social media transformed marketing from broadcast (one-to-many) to relational (many-to-many) modes.

Theoretical Basis

Goods-Dominant Logic (G-D logic) — the traditional paradigm:

  • Value is created by the producer and embedded in tangible goods
  • Exchange involves transfer of ownership
  • Consumers are passive recipients of value propositions
  • Marketing delivers messages to audiences

Service-Dominant Logic — the revised paradigm:

  • Value is co-created through the consumption process
  • Consumers actively participate in creating meaning
  • The firm offers value propositions; consumers evaluate and create value in their own terms
  • Marketing facilitates ongoing relationships and exchange

Vargo & Lusch (2004) articulated S-D logic through several foundational premises:

  1. Service is the fundamental basis of exchange (not goods)
  2. Value is always uniquely and phenomenologically determined by the beneficiary
  3. The customer is always a co-creator of value
  4. All economic actors are resource integrators

S-D logic draws on service marketing, relationship marketing (Grönroos 1994, 2004), and theories of consumer co-production.

How It Works

Why Social Media Made S-D Logic Salient

Social media's defining characteristics — interactivity, user-generated content, many-to-many communication — naturally align with S-D logic. Traditional media (TV, print, radio) was goods-dominant: content was produced, audiences received it. Social media inverts this: the platform enables (and requires) consumer participation. The content a fan sees is partly produced by other fans.

This is why Filo et al. (2015) adopted S-D logic as the organising framework for sport social media research: social media operationalises the co-creation that S-D logic describes.

Relationship Marketing as Derivative

Relationship marketing — "the efforts of brands to build and maintain bonds with customers and other stakeholders through mutual exchange and interactivity" (Grönroos 2004) — is a practical expression of S-D logic. If value is co-created through interaction, then marketing must facilitate ongoing relationships rather than deliver one-off messages.

In sport: ticket sales, merchandise, and broadcast deals are transactional (G-D logic). Engaging fans in creating meaning together — through content, community, co-production — is relational (S-D logic).

Contested Aspects

  • Practitioner translation: S-D logic is conceptually clear but operationally abstract. What does "co-creation" mean in practical social media management? The concept can become a buzzword without substance.

  • Not all interactions are co-creation: Scrolling, viewing, or even liking may not constitute the active value creation S-D logic describes. The framework risks over-claiming that any interaction is co-creative.

  • Measurement challenge: If value is "uniquely and phenomenologically determined by the beneficiary," how can organisations measure whether co-creation is occurring or succeeding?

  • Asymmetric application: S-D logic emphasises consumer agency, but platforms and brands still control architecture, algorithms, and access. The "co" in co-creation may overstate consumer power.

Gaps

  • Empirical application to fan development: S-D logic in sport remains largely conceptual. No study has tested whether co-creative engagement actually produces attachment or allegiance.

  • Does co-creation produce different fan types?: S-D logic describes that value is co-created but not what kind of relationship results. Does co-creation produce traditional/supporter attachment or consumer/fan attachment (per Giulianotti)? Unclear.

  • Platform effects: Social media algorithms increasingly determine what content users see. Does algorithmic mediation undermine the co-creative premise? S-D logic predates the algorithmic feed era.

  • Cross-cultural validity: S-D logic emerged from Western marketing scholarship. Consumer-producer relationships and the meaning of "value" may differ across cultural contexts.

Applied Note

S-D logic provides the conceptual vocabulary for escaping vanity metrics in sport social media strategy. The goods-dominant reflex is to measure reach, impressions, and followers — how many people received the message. The S-D logic question is different: What value did fans create through their engagement?

From "Did They See It?" to "Did They Participate?"

G-D Logic Question S-D Logic Question
How many impressions? How many created content?
How many followers? How many participated in discussion?
How many likes? How many contributed to community meaning?
What was the reach? What value did fans co-create?

This reframing has practical implications:

  1. Content strategy: Design posts that invite participation (polls, challenges, user-generated content campaigns) rather than passive consumption. The post that generates comments and shares may matter more than the post with high reach but no interaction.

  2. Community management: Responding to comments, amplifying fan content, and facilitating fan-to-fan connection are co-creation enablers — not just good customer service.

  3. Success metrics: Track engagement quality (depth of interaction, content creation, community formation) alongside engagement quantity.

  4. Fan development: Co-creative engagement may provide the social bonding and identity-linking that the PCM identifies as necessary for moving fans from attraction to attachment. S-D logic suggests why engagement might facilitate this transition — value created together produces meaning attached to the relationship.

The caveat: S-D logic is a marketing paradigm, not a fan development theory. It explains why relational marketing matters but does not specify how co-creation produces psychological commitment. The integration with PCM that Filo et al. (2015) recommend remains to be tested.
