Becoming fans abroad: a qualitative study of soccer fan socialisation

Core Claim

Adults who were not socialised into soccer fandom during childhood can become fans through cultural immersion while travelling to places where soccer is central to local culture. For these individuals, becoming a fan satisfies simultaneous needs for belonging (feeling part of the local culture) and uniqueness (experiencing traditions thrillingly different from home sports culture). This is the first empirical study to document a specific adult conversion pathway outside the new team formation context.

Key Findings / Arguments

  • Three socialisation pathways identified: family (childhood), peers (childhood/adolescence), and cultural immersion via travel (adulthood). Of 22 participants, 9 were socialised as adults via cultural immersion while travelling abroad. (Table 1, p. 318)

  • Cultural immersion as adult conversion mechanism: Adults who were not childhood soccer fans became fans through exposure during travel (typically study abroad) to places where soccer was central to local culture — England, Italy, Scotland. (pp. 321-324)

  • Belonging motive: Becoming a fan while abroad provided a sense of belonging with locals. "It was just something to do with them" — fandom enabled social integration into the foreign culture. (pp. 322-323)

  • Uniqueness motive: The traditions and rituals of European soccer were perceived as thrillingly different from US sport culture. Participants were drawn to what was different — the singing, the intensity, the cultural embeddedness. "It's a great spectacle, and it's more than just a game." (pp. 323-324)

  • Active subcultural immersion required: Socialisation occurred because individuals immersed themselves in the sport subculture — learning supporter songs, dressing appropriately, interacting with passionate fans. Passive exposure was insufficient. (pp. 325-326)

  • Fandom maintenance challenge: Once returning to the USA, individuals socialised via travel struggled to maintain fandom. The socialising agent (the immersive culture) became distant; they became "pseudo-displaced fans" who could only follow virtually. Some fandom "fell" upon return. (p. 324)

  • Prior sport fandom predisposition: Those converted via travel were already fans of other sports before travelling. General sport fandom may predispose individuals to acquire new specific fandom. (p. 321)

Methods

Three focus groups with 22 soccer fans (16 male, 6 female, ages 20-32) recruited from undergraduate and graduate business classes at a large US northeastern university. Participants represented six countries. Concept mapping activity guided discussion. Qualitative analysis using constant comparison method.

Sample limitations: Small convenience sample; young adults primarily; recruited from academic setting; soccer-specific; US-resident participants travelling to Europe; retrospective self-report.

Contributions to Fandom Theory

This is the most direct empirical treatment of adult fan origination in the corpus. Its contributions:

  1. Documents a specific adult conversion pathway: Cultural immersion via travel is the first empirically identified mechanism for adult fan acquisition outside the new team formation context.

  2. Moves beyond childhood socialisation paradigm: Provides evidence that adult conversion is real, systematic, and explicable — not just an exception to the childhood-origin rule.

  3. Identifies dual motive structure: Belonging + uniqueness as simultaneous drivers maps onto Optimal Distinctiveness Theory (Brewer 1991), though the paper frames this through SIT. This provides a theoretical handle on why adult conversion occurs via cultural immersion.

  4. Reveals durability challenge: Adult-acquired fandom via travel may be fragile — the socialising agent (immersive culture) is removed upon return home. This has implications for understanding whether adult-origin fandom is structurally different from childhood-origin fandom.

  5. Extends place identity research: Prior work (Heere & Katz 2014) showed place identity as socialising agent for home location; this extends it to visited locations.

Limitations & Caveats

  • Qualitative and small sample: N=22, focus group methodology. Findings are exploratory and suggestive, not generalisable.

  • Soccer-specific: Travel conversion may work for sports embedded in local culture abroad but not for all sports.

  • Globalisation context: Findings specific to Americans travelling to Europe for study abroad. May not apply to other travel contexts or directions.

  • Young adult sample: Primarily 20-32 year olds in academic settings. Does not capture adult conversion at other life stages (mid-career, retirement).

  • Retrospective self-report: Participants recounting how they became fans; subject to reconstruction bias.

  • No comparison group: Does not compare travel-converted fans with fans socialised via other pathways on outcomes (identification level, durability, behaviour).

  • Cross-sectional: Does not track conversion process over time or fandom maintenance longitudinally.

Applied Note

For Sporting Cultural Architecture, this paper has direct implications for designing entry-point experiences for new adult audiences:

The cultural immersion insight: Adults convert when they are immersed in contexts where:

  1. The sport is culturally embedded and locally meaningful (not just available but central)
  2. They can feel belonging with locals through shared participation
  3. The experience is perceived as authentically unique/different

Implications for experience design:

  • First-time adult attendees need immersive experiences, not passive observation. They need to learn songs, wear colours, interact with passionate fans — to feel like insiders, not tourists.
  • The "uniqueness" motive suggests differentiation matters: experiences must be distinctively different from other entertainment options the adult has access to.
  • The "belonging" motive suggests social scaffolding matters: first-time adults need companions, hosts, or facilitated entry into fan communities — not just tickets.

The maintenance challenge: Properties cannot assume a single immersive experience will produce durable fandom. Post-experience engagement must substitute for the ongoing cultural immersion that travel naturally provided. This may require: ongoing digital engagement, connection to local fan communities, return visit incentives, and active community management for "distant fans."

For properties entering new markets: The study abroad context is analogous to international expansion. Properties should consider how to create immersive cultural moments in new markets — not just broadcast availability but live event experiences, local fan community seeding, and partnerships that make the sport feel locally embedded rather than foreign import.
