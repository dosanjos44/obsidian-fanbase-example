This file governs how this wiki is structured, maintained, and queried.

When in doubt, consult this document. When this document is wrong, update it.

Purpose of This Wiki

This is a personal knowledge base on the sociology and social psychology of sports fandom, with particular focus on fan origination — how people become fans — split across two distinct pathways:

  • Childhood origination: socialisation, family transmission, cognitive development, identity formation
  • Adult origination: conversion events, life-stage triggers, migration/diaspora, new teams, social network effects

A secondary purpose is grounding applied thinking in Sporting Cultural Architecture — a personal framework for sports marketing, fan development, and format/experience design. Academic sources inform this applied layer; syntheses bridge between them.

The human (Jamie) curates sources, directs analysis, and asks questions. The LLM reads, writes, cross-references, and maintains. Jamie never edits wiki pages directly — all changes go through the LLM so consistency is maintained.

Page Formats

sources/

Filename: [firstauthor-year-slug].md
Examples: james-2001-cognitive-development-team-loyalty.md, giulianotti-2002-supporters-followers-fans-flaneurs.md

Frontmatter (YAML, always present):

---
title: "Full paper title"
authors: ["Last, First", "Last, First"]
year: 2004
journal: "Journal of Sport Management"
tradition: sport_sociology | sport_management | psychology | fan_studies | consumer_research | general_sociology
pathway: childhood | adult | general | both
fandom_type: identity | social | entertainment | mixed
source_file: raw/james-2001-cognitive-development-team-loyalty.pdf
status: ingested | stub
---

Body sections (use exactly these headings):

## Core Claim
One paragraph. The single most important thing this paper argues or finds.
No hedging. State it directly.

## Key Findings / Arguments
Bullet list. Specific, citable claims with page numbers where possible.
No more than 8 bullets — if there are more, only the most important.

## Methods
Brief. Study design, sample, context. Flag if sample is narrow (e.g. US undergrads only).

## Contributions to Fandom Theory
How does this paper advance understanding? What does it add that wasn't there before?

## Limitations & Caveats
Be honest. Methodological weaknesses, sample issues, generalisability problems.
This is important — the literature has real weaknesses and they should be visible.

## Connections
Links to concept pages this paper develops or touches.
Links to other source pages that agree, contradict, or extend this work.
Format: [[concept/team-identification]], [[source/wann-1993-ssis]]

## Applied Note
One paragraph. What does this mean for sports marketing, fan development,
or Sporting Cultural Architecture thinking? Bridge from academic to applied.
If not applicable, write "Not directly applicable at this stage."

concepts/

Filename: [concept-slug].md
Examples: team-identification.md, psychological-continuum-model.md, reverse-socialisation.md, childhood-origination.md

Frontmatter:

---
title: "Concept Name"
aliases: ["alternative names", "abbreviations"]  # e.g. ["SSIS", "identification scale"]
tradition: sport_sociology | sport_management | psychology | fan_studies | consumer_research | general_sociology | multiple
pathway: childhood | adult | general | both
fandom_type: identity | social | entertainment | mixed | n/a
key_sources: ["james-2001-cognitive-development", "funk-james-2001-pcm"]
status: developed | stub | contested
---

Body sections:

## Definition
Clear, precise definition in plain language. No jargon unless defined.
Note where the concept originates (author, year).

## Theoretical Basis
What larger theory or framework does this concept sit within?
(e.g. Social Identity Theory, Piaget's developmental stages, figurational sociology)

## How It Works
Mechanism. If it's a process, describe the stages. If it's a typology, describe the types.
Use concrete examples where possible.

## Key Sources
Who developed, tested, criticised, or extended this concept?
List with brief notes on each source's contribution.
Format: [[source/wann-1993-ssis]] — introduced the Sport Spectator Identification Scale

## Contested Aspects
Where do scholars disagree? What are the known critiques?
This section must be populated honestly — do not elide disagreement.

## Relationship to Adjacent Concepts
How does this connect to related concepts in the wiki?
Format: [[concept/identity-fusion]] — a deeper form of identification; see also [[concept/birging]]

## Gaps
What does this concept not explain? Where does the literature run out?

## Applied Note
What does this concept imply for fan development, marketing, or format design?
Concrete and direct. One to two paragraphs.

Body: free-form but should include: why this entity matters, what they are known for, key associated concepts and sources, and any important caveats (e.g. "Wann's sample base is heavily US undergraduate — generalise with caution").

syntheses/

The highest-value pages. These are compound analyses that cut across multiple sources and concepts. They can be generated from queries ("file this answer as a synthesis") or created proactively.

Filename: [slug].md
Examples: childhood-vs-adult-origination-compared.md, what-literature-says-about-womens-sport-fan-acquisition.md, sporting-cultural-architecture-fan-development-implications.md

Frontmatter:

---
title: "Synthesis title"
created: YYYY-MM-DD
sources_drawn_on: ["james-2001", "crawford-2004", "delia-katz-2019"]
type: comparison | literature_review | applied_analysis | framework
---

Body: free-form. The LLM writes it; Jamie reviews and can annotate with > [Jamie's note: ...] blockquotes. These annotations are preserved in future edits.

Rule: every synthesis must end with a ## Open Questions section — the things this synthesis reveals that remain unresolved. These feed the next round of reading and querying.

gaps/

Short pages documenting where the literature is demonstrably thin, contested, or absent. These guide future reading acquisition.

Filename: [slug].md
Examples: adult-fan-conversion-mechanisms.md, re-engagement-lapsed-fans.md, non-western-fandom-formation.md

Body:

## What's Missing
Precise description of the gap.

## Why It Matters
Why does this gap matter for theory or for applied work?

## Partial Coverage
Sources that partially address this, and what they leave out.

## Suggested Reading Directions
What kinds of sources might fill this gap? Are there adjacent fields
(developmental psychology, migration studies, consumer socialisation)
that have relevant work not yet in the corpus?

Taxonomy Reference

Use these exact values in frontmatter — no variations.

tradition

  • sport_sociology — figurational, post-structural, ethnographic; Elias/Dunning, Giulianotti, Crawford
  • sport_management — quantitative, managerial; Wann, Funk, James, Trail, Heere
  • psychology — social/developmental psychology; Tajfel/Turner, SIT, Piaget
  • fan_studies — Jenkins, Sandvoss, Gray; media-fan crossover
  • consumer_research — Muñiz/O'Guinn brand community, McAlexander, John (consumer socialisation)
  • general_sociology — Putnam, Oldenburg, Maffesoli, Anderson; not sport-specific

pathway

  • childhood — fan origination through developmental socialisation, family, school
  • adult — fan origination through conversion events, life-stage triggers, migration, relocation
  • general — not specific to either pathway (e.g. motivation scales applied to existing fans)
  • both — explicitly addresses both pathways and/or the transition between them

fandom_type

  • identity — fandom as constituent of self-concept; team identification, BIRGing/CORFing, fusion
  • social — fandom as community and belonging; sense of community, brand community, third place
  • entertainment — fandom as consumption, leisure, aesthetic; motives scales, flâneur, media fandom
  • mixed — explicitly integrates two or more types
  • n/a — not applicable (for general sociology concepts)

status (concepts only)

  • developed — well-covered by sources in the corpus; concept page is substantive
  • stub — page exists but needs more sources to develop properly
  • contested — genuine theoretical disagreement; ## Contested Aspects must be detailed

Ingest Workflow

When Jamie drops a new PDF into /raw/ and says "ingest [filename]":

  1. Read the full source document
  2. Brief discussion with Jamie: "Here are the 3 most important things this paper contributes — does this match your read?"
  3. Write sources/[slug].md following the format above
  4. Identify all concepts the paper touches. For each:
    • If concept page exists: update it (add this source to Key Sources, update any sections the paper develops, add/update Contested Aspects if relevant)
    • If concept page doesn't exist: create it as a stub, then develop it using this source
  5. Create or update entity pages for authors if they are major figures in the field
  6. Update index.md
  7. Append to log.md
  8. Tell Jamie: "Ingested. Created N pages, updated M pages. Concepts touched: [list]. Gaps flagged: [list if any]."

Do not batch-ingest without Jamie present. Ingest one source at a time with a brief discussion step.

Query Workflow

When Jamie asks a question:

  1. Read index.md to identify relevant pages
  2. Read relevant concept, source, and synthesis pages
  3. Synthesise an answer with inline citations — always cite [Author Year] format
  4. Ask: "Should I file this as a synthesis page?" If yes, write it to syntheses/[slug].md
  5. Append query to log.md

Citation format in answers: [James 2001] for in-text, full reference at the bottom.

Lint Workflow

Run periodically (every 10–15 ingests, or when Jamie asks):

  1. Check for orphan pages (no inbound links from index or other pages)
  2. Check for contradictions between concept pages and source pages
  3. Check for stale claims (a newer source supersedes an older one but the concept page hasn't been updated)
  4. Identify important concepts mentioned across pages but lacking their own concept page
  5. Review all gaps/ pages — have any been partially addressed by new ingests?
  6. Suggest 3–5 sources to acquire next, based on current gaps
  7. Append lint summary to log.md

Sporting Cultural Architecture Layer

This wiki serves not just academic understanding but applied thinking under the personal framework Sporting Cultural Architecture — the design of sports experiences, competitions, and fan development strategies grounded in sociological reality rather than superficial marketing convention.

Applied Notes on source and concept pages should always ask:

  • What does this imply for how a sports property acquires new fans (vs. just activating existing ones)?
  • What does this imply for the design of the live experience as a socialisation environment?
  • What does this imply for the difference between childhood and adult fan development strategies?
  • What does this challenge about common sports marketing assumptions?

Synthesis pages in the sporting-cultural-architecture cluster should be explicit about bridging from academic claim to applied implication, without over-stretching the evidence.

Rules the LLM Must Always Follow

  1. Never modify files in /raw/ — these are immutable source documents.
  2. Always update index.md and log.md after any ingest or synthesis filing.
  3. Preserve Jamie's annotations — blockquotes marked > [Jamie's note: ...] are never overwritten.
  4. Be honest about limitations — every source page must have a ## Limitations & Caveats section. Do not write flattering summaries of weak methodology.
  5. Flag contradictions explicitly — when a new source contradicts an existing concept page, update the ## Contested Aspects section and note the disagreement in the log.
  6. One ingest at a time — never batch-process sources without Jamie present.
  7. Applied Notes are mandatory — every source and concept page must have one. The bridge from academic to applied is the point.
  8. Open Questions are mandatory — every synthesis page must end with ## Open Questions.
  9. Use exact taxonomy values — no invented tags. If something doesn't fit, discuss with Jamie before adding a new value.
  10. When uncertain, ask — do not silently make structural decisions. Surface them.
