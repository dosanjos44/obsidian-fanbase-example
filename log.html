Notes: Process model complementing Hyatt & Foster 2015. KEY CONTRIBUTION: Four-phase sequential model of detachment (breakdown trigger → iterative decline → disengagement incident → exit) drawn from customer-brand dissolution literature. Directly answers Funk & James's PCM call for downward movement research. APPLIED INSIGHT: Iterative decline phase is the intervention window — "restorative actions" (tailored tickets, responsive communication, secondary market facilitation) can interrupt the process before exit crystallises. Long-term holders show longer iterative decline (more intervention time, more accumulated grievances). COMPLEMENTS HYATT & FOSTER: Where Hyatt & Foster explain why fans exit (value misalignment vs practical barriers), Martin & Goldman explain how exit unfolds temporally. LIMITATIONS FLAGGED: Small sample (N=14), single franchise (South African Super Rugby), context-specific triggers (stadium safety, "pink jerseys"), retrospective methodology, no comparison with fans who repaired.

Notes: The most direct treatment of adult fan origination in the corpus. KEY FINDING: Adults become soccer fans through cultural immersion while travelling to places where the sport is culturally embedded. MECHANISM: Belonging (feeling part of local culture) + uniqueness (traditions differ from home) — maps onto Optimal Distinctiveness Theory. LIMITATIONS: Qualitative, N=22, soccer-specific, young adult study abroad context, US→Europe travel direction. Does not cover non-travel adult conversion pathways. APPLIED IMPLICATION: Adult conversion requires immersive experiences satisfying both belonging and uniqueness; passive exposure insufficient.

[2026-05-03] ingest | lock-heere-2017-identity-review

Pages created: sources/lock-heere-2017-identity-review.md, entities/lock-daniel.md
Pages updated: concepts/team-identification.md (substantially expanded Contested Aspects with IT/SIT conflation critique; added Lock & Heere to key_sources; updated Theoretical Basis, Gaps, and Applied Note), entities/ssis-scale.md (added detailed Lock & Heere critique section; updated item-by-item analysis), sources/wann-1993-ssis.md (expanded Limitations & Caveats with detailed Lock & Heere critique; added connection), index.md, log.md
Notes: The revisionist critique paper. Lock & Heere argue the field has conflated Identity Theory (role-based, Stryker 1968) and Social Identity Theory (group-based, Tajfel & Turner 1979) under the single construct "team identification" for 25+ years. SSIS items tap both frameworks inconsistently. KEY INSIGHT: SSIS has predictive validity but questionable construct validity — we don't know what it's actually measuring. PROPOSED SOLUTION: Distinguish "fan identification" (role-based) from "team identification" (group-based). APPLIED IMPLICATION: Different intervention strategies follow from each framework; the literature cannot guide which matters more because it never asked the question clearly. Does not invalidate prior research but serves as methodological health warning across all SSIS-based findings. LIMITATIONS: Theoretical, not empirical; no new measure provided; uncertainty whether the distinction matters practically.

Notes: The single most important paper on childhood origination. Uses Piagetian developmental framework (preoperational, transitional, concrete operational) to show psychological commitment can form from age 5.
